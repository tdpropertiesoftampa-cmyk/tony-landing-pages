What should I check before buying a lot in Tampa Bay?

Check zoning and future land use, whether water and sewer are available or you need a well and septic, the flood zone for that exact parcel, the survey and easements, and legal access to the lot. Rules differ by county and city, so confirm each item with the local planning office in writing.

What is the difference between zoning and future land use?

Zoning is what the rules allow on a property today, and future land use is what the county or city plans for the area long term. They do not always match, and that can limit what you can build or split, so ask the planning office about both.

Can I split a lot in Tampa Bay?

It depends on the county or city and the lot. Ask the planning office about the minimum lot size and width, the process and timeline, and any utility or impact fees, and get the answers in writing before you buy.

How do I calculate ARV for a flip?

Use recent sold homes, not active listings, that are close by and similar in size, style, and finished condition, then adjust for bedrooms, baths, lot, garage, and pool. It helps to write down a conservative, expected, and best-case number and plan your offer around the conservative one.

What costs do flippers forget?

Holding costs are the most common miss: property taxes, insurance, utilities, loan interest, and HOA fees for every month you own the house. Also count financing costs, selling costs, a repair contingency, and insurance, which can change with roof age and flood zone.

Why should I bring my own agent when buying new construction?

The sales representative at a model home works for the builder, and many builders ask whether you have an agent the first time you visit. Having your own agent means someone on your side is reviewing the contract and asking about incentives and what is included.

What fees should I ask about in a new community?

Ask about HOA fees, CDD fees, and any special assessments. A CDD is a community development district, and it can add a charge on top of the HOA, often on the tax bill. Get the amounts and how long they last in writing.

What are my options for selling a house that needs work?

You can list it as is, make a few repairs and then list, or sell directly to an investor or cash buyer. The best choice depends on how fast you need to sell, how much work the house needs, and what you would net in each case.

Do Florida sellers have to disclose known problems?

In general, Florida sellers are expected to disclose known problems that affect a home's value and are not easy for a buyer to see. This is general information, not legal advice, so ask your agent or a real estate attorney about your situation.
